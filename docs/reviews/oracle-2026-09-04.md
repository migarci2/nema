## 2. Devpost story and pitch

### The 200-character pitch

The current pitch is not ready because "One tag" is false and "any agent" is broader than the tested claim. 

Replace it with this, at **159 characters**:

> Learn it once. It counts everywhere. WebMCP lets teaching pages ask your vault what you know, with consent, and give you a signed receipt a later page can use.

It contains the problem, the enabling technology, the human gate and the portable outcome. It also avoids spending scarce characters on implementation inventory.

### The repeated plain-language layer

Keep the headline. It is memorable and now functions as the submission's organizing promise:

> **Learn it once. It counts everywhere.**

The headline must mean that the evidence of an event persists, not that one exposure creates permanent knowledge. The sentence under it should establish that boundary.

Replace:

> Learn something on one site, and the next one already knows. You decide what gets shared, every time.

With one sentence:

> Learn on one site, and another page that works with nema can start from the evidence you kept, using only what you approved for that site.

This removes three problems at once:

* A random next site does not "already know".
* The thing that moves is evidence, not omniscient knowledge.
* A remembered approval does not contradict "every time".

The current wording is repeated in the submission, guide, README and extension, so this must be one global replacement rather than a local Devpost edit.  

### Story passages that should be replaced

#### 1. The universal opening claim

Replace:

> Every site starts from zero, and nothing we did on one page survives on the next.

With:

> Independent teaching pages rarely share a learner record. When we move between them, the next page usually has no evidence of what we did before.

The existing sentence is rhetorically clean but literally false for ecosystems with shared accounts, standards or integrations.

#### 2. "Why this needs WebMCP"

The current section is a substantial improvement, but its final sentence still says any teaching page becomes operable by any agent. It also leaves receipt issuance outside the causal explanation. 

Replace the whole paragraph with:

> **Why this needs WebMCP.** A teaching page knows what it teaches. The learner's vault knows what evidence it holds. WebMCP lets the browser agent bring those two capabilities together while both pages are open: the page declares what it needs, the vault returns only the named status values under the learner's approval, and the page adapts. After a check, the page can issue a signed receipt back to the vault. Without WebMCP, an agent would need a custom integration for each site or an extension that interprets the page.

This is stronger because it explains not just communication, but why each capability belongs on its own page.

#### 3. The three beats

Replace:

> **A site asks.** You open a page that teaches something, and it asks what you already know before it teaches you anything.

With:

> **A site asks.** Before a teaching page adapts its path, it declares the readiness bands it needs.

Replace:

> **You say yes.** You see exactly what was asked, and only what you approve leaves.

With:

> **You decide.** The vault shows the requested fields, audience, purpose and expiry before it returns anything.

Replace:

> **It counts next time.** You do the work that is left, and what you did here counts on the next site.

With:

> **It counts next time.** After you pass a check, the site can issue a signed receipt that another participating page can use through your vault.

The current three-part structure is excellent. Keep the structure and make each sentence mechanically true.

#### 4. Ownership

The operational ownership details are good, but several surrounding phrases remain stronger than the mechanism supports. A signature does not prove that work "actually" happened, and the web vault does technically live on a site origin even though its data remains in local storage. 

Replace the full paragraph with:

> **A vault you own.** The vault is your own ledger of learning evidence: signed receipts for activities and checks that sites recorded for you. It derives coarse bands, review dates and next-step suggestions from that ledger. The keys are created in your browser, the ledger exports as one file, and you can erase the vault. Teaching providers do not hold the complete record.

This preserves the impact while separating a signed record from a verified educational truth.

#### 5. The provider installation

Replace:

> **A protocol a site installs in a minute.** Two tags on any page.
> That page now exposes six WebMCP tools: it describes what it teaches, asks the vault about three prerequisites, adapts, and signs a receipt when the reader passes a check. No backend needed.

With:

> **A small protocol for teaching pages.** Add one manifest block and one script tag. Together they expose five imperative WebMCP tools and one declarative form. The page can describe what it teaches, accept a scoped readiness assertion, adapt its path, and issue a receipt after a check. The self-certified tier needs no backend.

This fixes "one tag", explains the six entries honestly, and scopes the no-backend claim to the self-certified tier.

#### 6. The learner policy

The current paragraph is directionally right, especially the sentence that calls it an inspectable policy. Two details should be softened: prerequisite credit is conditional on the declared graph, and the constants are not literally all in one file. 

Replace:

> Passing something advanced credits every prerequisite under it at a fraction and pushes its review date out.

With:

> Passing something advanced can credit declared prerequisites at a fraction and extend their review dates.

Replace the final two sentences with:

> It is an inspectable policy, not a psychometric model. Every weight, decay rule and threshold is a documented demo default in the repository. A signature proves which key issued a receipt and that its payload was not altered. It does not prove how difficult or valid the check was.

#### 7. Consent

Replace:

> **Consent you can see.** A site never reads the vault. It asks one question, and the vault shows a modal: what will be shared (three status bands), what will not (history, scores, other subjects), for which site, for how long. You approve. The answer is a signed token bound to that one site, valid thirty minutes.

With:

> **Consent you can see.** A teaching page never reads the vault directly. It names the bands it needs. The first approval shows the requested fields, audience, purpose and expiry. Every signed answer is bound to that site and expires after thirty minutes.

This accommodates both the direct three-item agent request and the extension's broader request without pretending the number is universal. It also accommodates remembered approval without saying a modal appears every time.

#### 8. The two courses

Replace:

> **Two example courses that look nothing like nema.** Saucier School (pan sauces) asks your vault three things and turns 68 minutes into 27, then 21. Line Cook Lab (food safety on the pass) has never spoken to Saucier School, but recognises what you did there and unlocks its advanced lab.

With:

> **Two example courses that look nothing like nema.** Saucier School turns a 68 minute path into 27 after the learner approves its readiness request. After the ratios check is passed and a fresh assertion is presented, 27 becomes 21. Line Cook Lab has never spoken to Saucier School, but it recognises evidence from the learner's vault, skips material already covered, and names the prerequisite still missing.

The arithmetic is supported by the contract, but 21 requires a second assertion after the receipt. Line Cook should not be credited with an unlock caused by a separate assessment. 

#### 9. The mirrored articles

Replace:

> **Two real articles, with and without nema.** We mirrored a CC BY-SA article on AES-GCM and an MIT chapter of "Putting the You in CPU", added retrieval questions inside the text, and put the original and the nema version side by side with the exact diff as the headline.

With:

> **Two real articles, with and without nema.** We republished frereit's AES-GCM article under CC BY-SA 4.0 and Lexi Mattick's CPU chapter under its MIT licence, with attribution and change notices. We added retrieval questions and show each original beside the nema version with the generated diff.

"Republished under" is more precise than "mirrored", and naming the authors makes the attribution visible without forcing a judge to inspect the footer.

#### 10. Agents

Replace:

> **Any agent.** ChatGPT desktop and Chrome 149 call the tools on the page. Claude Code and Codex reach the same vault over MCP (`packages/nema-mcp`, the same eleven tools, same schemas). The agent is a commodity; the vault is the infrastructure.

With:

> **The agent you already use.** ChatGPT's in-app browser and an agent in WebMCP-enabled Chrome can call the tools on the page. Claude Code and Codex reach the eleven imperative vault tools over MCP. nema does not require its own agent; the learner's vault is the persistent part.

Chrome itself is not the agent, "any agent" is too broad, and "commodity" sounds dismissive in a public product story.

#### 11. The extension

Replace:

> **nema in your browser.** A Chrome extension puts the vault in the side panel and does the boring transport with no model: a bar appears on any site that works with nema, one click shares your bands, and receipts are collected automatically when you pass something.

With:

> **nema in your browser.** The Chrome extension puts the vault in a side panel and handles the transport with no model. Its page bar opens the same approval step, and after the learner passes a check, an accepted receipt is collected into the local vault.

This preserves the attractive zero-copy interaction without claiming disclosure is one click.

#### 12. Local concept names

Replace:

> **Sites that speak their own names.** A site can use its own concept ids. An agent proposes what they mean, you confirm once, and the vault translates at its edges.

With:

> **Sites can keep their own names.** A site may declare its own vocabulary. An agent may propose a mapping for an undeclared name, but only the learner can confirm or reject it. Alignments translate names without changing who signed a receipt or how much the evidence weighs.

The current implementation can auto-confirm a provider's declaration about its own vocabulary, so the agent is not always the proposer.

#### 13. The six protocol objects

Replace:

> The protocol is six objects (...) carried as compact tokens.

With:

> The protocol defines six objects. Readiness assertions and evidence receipts use compact ECDSA P-256 signed tokens. Manifests, requests and learning needs use JSON; alignments remain inspectable records in the vault.

The present sentence wrongly implies that all six objects are signed compact tokens.

#### 14. Browser support

Replace:

> The same code runs natively in Chrome 149+ and ChatGPT desktop and degrades gracefully elsewhere.

With:

> Each page registers its tools with `document.modelContext.registerTool`. Native end-to-end tests ran on Chrome for Testing 154. The project targets WebMCP-enabled Chrome 149+ and ChatGPT's in-app browser; the bundled Chrome Labs polyfill provides a degraded demonstration path elsewhere.

This cleanly separates the environment actually tested from the compatibility target. The contract records native testing on Chrome for Testing 154 and a separate claim about the Chrome 149 flag. 

#### 15. Verification

Do not publish a hard-coded count until the final run. Replace the current sentence with a frozen result:

> At commit `<short-sha>`, `npm test` reports `<actual count>` passing unit tests. Separate native end-to-end suites cover the browser flow, connect handshake, extension, tampering, replay, wrong audience and expiry on Chrome for Testing 154.

Replace:

> Everything checkable: every claim in this text has a test that drives a real browser against the live URL.

With:

> The critical protocol paths are covered by unit tests and native browser runs against the live origins.

Licensing, pedagogical impact, originality and every prose statement plainly do not have browser tests.

#### 16. The required UX answer

Replace the paragraph beginning "You never start from zero" with:

> A participating site can start from relevant evidence already in the vault. It asks for a small set of readiness bands, and the vault returns one plain status per item. After a passed check, the page can issue a signed receipt for that event. In the normal browser path, the popup or extension carries the cryptographic material, so the learner does not have to copy it.

This removes the fixed count, the universal claim and "proof you understood it".

#### 17. Prior work and article changes

Replace:

> Everything was created during the submission period (...) with attribution, unchanged except for the nema additions shown in their diffs.

With:

> Everything original to nema was created during the submission period. The mirrored works retain their own licences and attribution. The comparison pages show the nema additions; the CPU mirror also removes analytics and fixes relative links, as disclosed on the page.

The contract itself says the CPU copy removes analytics and adjusts relative links, so "unchanged except for nema" is inaccurate.

## 4. The extension's three-state design

### Overall verdict

The extension is much calmer than the version described in the first review. The three-state model is understandable, machine details are behind one fold, and the main action is connected to the page currently open.

It is not yet literally "three states and nothing else". The normal surface can also contain a separate Next card, an alignment decision, receipt rows, a band-change sentence and a thirty-day remembered-approval control. The contract itself lists these additions after declaring that the panel has three states and nothing else. 

My judgment from the source rather than a rendered screenshot is:

* **Visual and tonal calmness:** likely good.
* **Conceptual calmness:** still one layer too busy.
* **Judge installability:** good, with three likely points of confusion.

### State 1: not a nema page

The fresh state is strong:

> Learn it once. It counts everywhere.
> Learn anywhere you see the nema mark.
> Load the demo learner.

The problem is the existing-vault version, where the away state can be accompanied by the full Next card. That means the judge sees a page-state surface and a learner-policy surface at once.

Preferred normal state:

> **Learn it once. It counts everywhere.**
> Open a page with the nema mark. It will ask here before anything is shared.

Move the Next recommendation under "Under the hood". It is a real feature, but it is not part of the extension's central proof.

### State 2: the page is asking

Current copy:

> Saucier School asks to know 5 things.

Replace it with:

> Saucier School asks about 5 things you may already know.

"Asks to know" sounds invasive and unnatural. "Asks about" describes the scoped request more accurately.

Map machine ability names into human phrases:

| Protocol ability | Extension wording  |
| ---------------- | ------------------ |
| recognize        | recognise          |
| retrieve         | from memory        |
| explain          | in your own words  |
| apply            | in practice        |
| transfer         | in a new situation |
| discriminate     | tell apart         |

So a row should read:

```text
Ratios, in practice                         not sure yet
```

not:

```text
Ratios, apply                               not sure yet
```

The current implementation renders the raw ability next to the concept and wraps the status in a pill, despite the current contract saying the three states should not contain a machine-word pill. 

Rename the first action:

* `Share` becomes **Review request**
* `Not now` becomes **Not on this visit**

That makes the two-step sequence coherent:

1. Review request
2. Approve or Deny

At present, "Share" implies the data leaves immediately, but it actually opens a separate approval step.

Replace the consent preview line:

> Shared for 30 minutes. Nothing else leaves.

With:

> This answer will be valid for 30 minutes. Nothing else will leave.

The current line is written in the past tense before approval has occurred. 

### The thirty-day checkbox

This is the most confusing affordance in the panel.

The same consent surface currently says:

* The answer lasts thirty minutes.
* Remember this site for thirty days.

Technically, those are different promises: each signed answer expires after thirty minutes, while the approval policy permits future answers for thirty days. A first-time judge is unlikely to infer that distinction.

My preferred deadline decision is to move the checkbox under "Under the hood". It is not necessary to demonstrate the core protocol and it contradicts the repeated "every time" language.

If it must remain visible, use:

> Remember my approval for this site for 30 days

and add:

> Each answer still expires after 30 minutes.

Do not leave the two bare durations beside one another.

### State 3: shared

The opening line is excellent:

> Shared with Saucier School. 68 minutes became 27.

Keep it.

For receipts, change the user-facing status from "verified" to "kept". "Verified" invites the interpretation that nema verified the learner's knowledge, while the extension has actually verified and accepted a signed receipt.

Recommended state:

```text
Shared with Saucier School. 68 minutes became 27.

What you did here

Ratios diagnostic                              kept

Ratios is now usable.
```

Move the full transition:

```text
ratios, apply: uncertain to usable, and 3 below it
```

under the hood. The code currently generates this more technical band-difference sentence for the main result. 

Replace:

> Nothing yet. Pass something on this page and it lands in your vault on its own.

With:

> Nothing yet. Pass something here and nema will keep the receipt.

The latter is still plain, but it makes the automatic action feel intentional rather than mysterious.

### What I would cut further

In order:

1. **Move the thirty-day approval control under the hood.**
2. **Move the Next rubric and Done self-check under the hood.** A self-reported Done button weakens the much stronger signed-evidence story when it appears immediately after a provider receipt.
3. **Keep alignment decisions under the hood during the normal course demo.** Surface them only on the article used to demonstrate vocabulary alignment.
4. **Reduce band movement to one sentence.** Keep the full ability-level diff below the fold.
5. **Render status as quiet text rather than a pill.**

Do not delete the functionality. The one closed technical surface is the right place for it.

### Judge-installation traps

Add these two warnings before the extension installation steps.

> This prototype runs on all pages so it can detect published nema tools. Before you choose Share, it reads only the tool list and the learning offer those tools expose.

The `<all_urls>` permission is already disclosed, but only much later in the extension README. A judge will see Chrome's permission warning before reaching that explanation. 

> The extension keeps a local vault for this browser profile. Load the demo learner inside the panel; the web vault and extension vault do not sync automatically.

The extension README says the two vaults are separate, but again only in the prototype section. A judge who loaded the web demo learner first may otherwise think the extension lost the data. 

Under the hood, add:

> Page transport: native WebMCP
> Panel transport: extension broker

The panel's own `chrome-extension://` page uses the polyfill because native WebMCP does not run there. The teaching page in the active tab can still be using native WebMCP. Without that distinction, a technical judge may inspect the panel and wrongly conclude the whole extension demonstration is polyfilled. 

Finally, smoke-test closing and reopening the side panel. The current `sharedWith` and `declined` state is held in in-memory sets. A panel reload can therefore forget that the current page was already shared or declined.  Only add session persistence if this failure reproduces in the final build.
