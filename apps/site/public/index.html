What we believe

Learning happens everywhere.

You learn from a video at midnight, from a friend who shows you once, from a bad night that you never want to repeat. Only a small part of it happens inside a course. Anything that counts only what one platform watched you do is measuring the wrong thing.

Evidence beats grades.

A number like 78 percent is a claim with no author. You cannot check it, argue with it or rebuild it if someone changes the formula. A plain record of what you did, who watched you do it and when, survives all three.

Memory needs you to come back.

Everything fades, on a schedule that is roughly predictable. The useful question is not what you once knew, it is what you could still do tonight. Something should notice when a skill is going quiet and bring it back before it goes.

Your learning state is yours.

The picture of what you can do is the most valuable thing your studying produces, and it is the one thing you usually cannot take with you. It should live where you live and move when you move. Cancelling a subscription should not erase you.

The web teaches, one site at a time.

The good teaching is spread across thousands of small sites that will never merge, partner or share a database. They should not have to. It is enough for a site to be able to ask you one question and get one honest answer.

An agent should coach, not judge.

An assistant that can write into your record can also lie about you, quietly and at scale. So it should have no way to do it. It can explain, plan, ask and encourage. Only you can answer, and only you decide what a site is allowed to know.

How it feels

I spent an evening on a cooking site learning pan sauces. It already knew I was fine with a knife and a hot pan, so it crossed out the two lessons that would have taught me that again and said why next to each one. What was left was the part I actually came for: why a butter sauce splits, and how to bring it back. At the end it handed me a small signed note of what I had done.

A week later I opened a completely different site, one about holding a busy service. The two sites have never spoken. It asked my vault three questions, I read them and said yes, and it quietly greyed out its own two introductory lessons and opened the lab I wanted, with a line saying the groundwork had been recognised from somewhere else. Nobody signed a partnership for that to happen.

This morning my agent told me I was about to lose something. I can brown meat beautifully, but I had never once shown that I could tell browning from caramelising, and the two were starting to blur. It asked me for the difference over coffee, four minutes, long before it mattered in front of a hot pan. It did not grade me. It just noticed.

What nema is

A vault. A page at an address you control, holding signed notes of work you have actually done and nothing else. It lives in your browser, not on our server, and it works out what you can do by reading those notes again every time you open it. There is no account, so there is nothing to cancel.

A protocol. A small, plain way for a website to ask your vault a question, and for your vault to give the shortest true answer. The site learns the few things it asked about and nothing else: no history, no scores, no other subjects, no dates. The answer is addressed to that one site, it expires in half an hour, and it does not exist until you say yes.

A coach. An agent that reads what the sites are offering, carries these notes between them and works through the gaps with you. It cannot answer a question in your place and it cannot write anything into your record, because there is no way to do either. It is good company, not an authority.

For builders and judges

Everything above is a claim, and the rest of this site exists so you can check it. The protocol reference has the objects, the token format, the verification rules and a ten point conformance list. The judge guide walks the whole demo in three minutes and lists the ways to try to break it. The repository is MIT, five small pages and no framework, so implementing a provider takes an afternoon. If you would rather read the argument than the schema, the philosophy is the long form of what we believe, and the brand board shows the system these pages are drawn from.
