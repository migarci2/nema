How to install nema

Prove it once. Skip it everywhere.

A site asks your vault what you already know, you say yes, and the site adapts. When you pass something, it signs a receipt that any other site can ask about. See the three beats on the hub.

You already teach something on the web: an article, a set of notes, a course. Two tags let a reader keep what they learned and let the next site recognise it. There is no backend to run and no account to make, and your page looks the way it looked before.

What you get

  • A quiet block on your page. The reader marks the piece read, answers a short check, and receives a signed receipt they can keep.
  • Five WebMCP tools for whatever agent the reader brought: describe_learning_offer, personalize_learning_path, start_activity, get_attempt_status, issue_evidence_receipt.
  • A path that shortens. When a reader presents a vault assertion, the parts they can skip are struck through with the reason beside each one.
  • The block inherits your fonts and your colours from the page it sits in.

Why the check at the end matters

Worth knowing before you write the questions: the argument Justin Skycak makes, and that Kris Abdelmessih collects in The Principles of Learning Fast, is that rereading produces familiarity and retrieval produces memory. Your reader closing the tab with a warm feeling is not the same as your reader knowing anything next month. Two questions they have to answer with the page scrolled away are worth more than another three paragraphs, and nema weights them that way: reading counts 0.1, a graded answer counts 1.0.

The manifest

{
  "protocol": "nema/0.1",
  "provider": { "name": "Maillard, explained" },
  "unit": {
    "id": "maillard-explained",
    "title": "Why browning tastes like that",
    "estimatedMinutes": 8
  },
  "requirements": [
    { "concept": "nema:heat-control", "ability": "explain" }
  ],
  "activities": [ ... ]
}

Put that JSON inside <script type="application/nema+json">, field by field:

  • protocol. Always nema/0.1.
  • provider.name. What a reader sees. Your origin is filled in for you.
  • unit.id. A slug, unique on your site. unit.title is what you call this piece, unit.estimatedMinutes an honest number.
  • requirements. What the piece assumes, as concept and ability pairs from shared/concepts.json. Leave it empty when it assumes nothing.
  • activities. What a reader can actually do here.

The activities block

  • A lesson renders one "Mark as read" button. Its grader is exposure, weight 0.1, which is honest: reading is not recall.
  • A quiz renders radio options and a Submit. Each question carries options and one answer id, so it grades deterministically in the page.
  • outcomes are the concept and ability pairs an activity is evidence for. skipIf is what a reader may skip once they present an assertion.

Several small quizzes beat one long one. A question after each section is a retrieval attempt while the section is still fresh, and a reader who gets one wrong finds out in four minutes rather than at the end of an exam they will not sit twice.

The script tag

<script type="module" src="https://nema.migarci2.dev/nema-provider.js"></script>

Put it after the manifest. The block renders where you place <nema-activities></nema-activities>, or at the end of your main or article if you place nothing.

Self certified receipts

The script makes a key pair in the reader's browser on first use and signs every receipt with it. The receipt carries keyId: "self:<origin>" and the public key, so anyone can verify it. A vault checks the signature, labels the receipt self, and caps its weight at the self report weight, 0.3, whatever grader you declared. You can vouch for yourself and only for yourself: enough to be useful, too little to be worth faking.

The two buttons that need no agent

Your readers do not have to install anything, and most of them will not have an agent. The block renders two buttons that do the whole loop with the browser alone.

  • Connect your vault. The reader's vault opens in a small window of its own, on its own origin, carrying your requirements plus every pair your skipIf rules read. The reader approves there, the vault posts the signed assertion back to your page, and the window closes. Your page then says what they can skip. You never see their vault, and the token never touches a clipboard.
  • Keep in my vault. After a pass, the same window opens with the receipt you just signed. The vault checks the signature, keeps it, and answers your page with the bands that moved, which your page prints in words: "Kept: browning science, now usable".

Popups are a top level window, which is the point: a vault inside an iframe would get a different, empty storage jar on every site, because browsers partition it. If a browser blocks the window, the block says so and points at the paste box underneath, which is the same code path.

Three optional upgrades

  • Point at another vault. Add data-vault="https://your-vault.example" to the script tag and both buttons talk to that origin instead. Default: https://nema-vault.migarci2.dev.
  • Sign on your server. Add data-endpoint="/api/receipt" to the script tag and the submission is posted to you, graded again and signed with your own key. apps/harness/worker.js in the repository is a working one.
  • Publish your key. Serve { "keyId", "jwk" } at /.well-known/nema-issuer.json with CORS *. A vault fetches it, matches it against the receipt and promotes that receipt to origin: full weight, still no partnership with anyone.

What readers see

Under your text, a small block in your own type: "Connect your vault", a button to mark the piece read, a check of two questions, and afterwards a receipt with "Keep in my vault". A reader with an agent never touches any of it, because the agent calls the tools. A reader with no agent presses the two buttons, or opens "Paste an assertion" and "Do it by hand" and carries the tokens themselves. Nothing there looks like nema except a 16 pixel mark and the words "Works with nema".
