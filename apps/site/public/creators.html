How to install nema

You already teach something on the web: an article, a set of notes, a course. Two tags let a reader keep what they learned with them, and let the next site recognise it. No backend, no account, no change to your design.

What you get

  • A quiet block on your page. The reader marks the piece read, answers a short check, and receives a signed receipt they can keep.
  • Five WebMCP tools for whatever agent the reader brought: describe_learning_offer, personalize_learning_path, start_activity, get_attempt_status, issue_evidence_receipt.
  • A path that shortens. When a reader presents a vault assertion, the parts they can skip are struck through with the reason beside each one.
  • Your fonts, your colours, your words. The block inherits the page it sits in.

The manifest

{
  "protocol": "nema/0.1",
  "provider": { "name": "Maillard, explained" },
  "unit": {
    "id": "maillard-explained",
    "title": "Why browning tastes like that",
    "estimatedMinutes": 8
  },
  "requirements": [
    { "concept": "nema:heat-control", "ability": "explain" }
  ],
  "activities": [ ... ]
}

Put that JSON inside <script type="application/nema+json">, field by field:

  • protocol. Always nema/0.1.
  • provider.name. What a reader sees. Your origin is filled in for you.
  • unit.id. A slug, unique on your site. unit.title is what you call this piece, unit.estimatedMinutes an honest number.
  • requirements. What the piece assumes, as concept and ability pairs from shared/concepts.json. Leave it empty when it assumes nothing.
  • activities. What a reader can actually do here.

The activities block

  • A lesson renders one "Mark as read" button. Its grader is exposure, weight 0.1, which is honest: reading is not recall.
  • A quiz renders radio options and a Submit. Each question carries options and one answer id, so it grades deterministically in the page.
  • outcomes are the concept and ability pairs an activity is evidence for. skipIf is what a reader may skip once they present an assertion.

The script tag

<script type="module" src="https://nema.migarci2.dev/nema-provider.js"></script>

Put it after the manifest. The block renders where you place <nema-activities></nema-activities>, or at the end of your main or article if you place nothing.

Self certified receipts

The script makes a key pair in the reader's browser on first use and signs every receipt with it. The receipt carries keyId: "self:<origin>" and the public key, so anyone can verify it. A vault checks the signature, labels the receipt self, and caps its weight at the self report weight, 0.3, whatever grader you declared. You can vouch for yourself and only for yourself: enough to be useful, too little to be worth faking.

Two optional upgrades

  • Sign on your server. Add data-endpoint="/api/receipt" to the script tag and the submission is posted to you, graded again and signed with your own key. apps/harness/worker.js in the repository is a working one.
  • Publish your key. Serve { "keyId", "jwk" } at /.well-known/nema-issuer.json with CORS *. A vault fetches it, matches it against the receipt and promotes that receipt to origin: full weight, still no partnership with anyone.

What readers see

Under your text, a small block in your own type: a button, a check of two questions, and afterwards a receipt with Copy and a "Send to vault" link. A reader with an agent never touches it, because the agent calls the tools. A reader with no agent uses the buttons, pastes an assertion into the textarea and watches the path shorten the same way. Nothing there looks like nema except a 16 pixel mark and the words "Works with nema".
